Anu Telugu Fonts: What They Are & Why Unicode Replaced Them

The fonts behind decades of Telugu DTP and printing — how they work, why the text breaks online, and what to use now.

8 min read·September 6, 2026·By Akshay

If you've ever received a Telugu document that opened as a jumble of English letters and symbols, you've met a legacy Telugu font. The best known of these is the Anu font family, used through Anu Script Manager — for decades the standard tool in Telugu DTP centres, printing presses and wedding-card shops.

This guide explains what Anu fonts are, why their text breaks outside the original software, and what you should use today.

What Are Anu Fonts?

Anu fonts are a family of Telugu display and text fonts used with Anu Script Manager, a commercial Telugu typing and DTP package. Before Unicode was widely supported, this approach was one of the only practical ways to produce good-looking Telugu in PageMaker, CorelDRAW and Photoshop, and it came with a large collection of decorative styles that printers loved.

How Legacy Telugu Fonts Work

Legacy fonts are ASCII (or "glyph-based") fonts. Instead of storing real Telugu characters, they reuse the codes of English letters and symbols and simply draw Telugu shapes in those slots. The typing software works out which pieces to place — a consonant here, a vowel sign there, a subscript below — and the font draws them.

The result looks perfect in the original file. But underneath, the computer only sees codes like "a", "k" or "]" — not Telugu.

History note: This wasn't unique to Anu. Many Telugu newspapers and publishers had their own non-Unicode fonts, and even Chathura — now a Unicode font on Google Fonts — started life in 2009 as one of 42 Telugu ASCII fonts in the Ezi Fonts collection.

Why Anu Text Breaks

  • No font, no Telugu. Open the file on a computer without that exact font and you see English gibberish.
  • Copy-paste fails. Pasting into WhatsApp, Gmail or a website pastes the hidden English codes.
  • Not searchable. Google and your computer's search can't find the words.
  • No spell-check or text-to-speech. Software can't tell it's Telugu.
  • Different fonts, different maps. Two legacy font families can use the same code for different letters, so text can't simply be switched from one to another.

Should You Still Use Anu Fonts?

If a print shop's whole workflow is built around them and the output only ever goes to paper, switching isn't always practical — and the decorative range is genuinely large. How to Move Old Anu Text to Unicode

  1. Check what you have. Open the file and note the font name. If it isn't a Unicode font (Gautami, Vani, Nirmala UI, Noto, Mandali…), it needs converting.
  2. Use a converter built for that font family. Newer versions of Anu Script Manager and several standalone tools can export or convert Anu text to Unicode. Because each legacy family uses its own mapping, a converter for one family won't work on another.
  3. Test on one paragraph first. Check conjuncts and vowel signs carefully — names and rare conjuncts are where conversions usually slip.
  4. Proofread. Always have a Telugu reader check the final text.
  5. For short text, retype. A few lines are often faster to retype with our Telugu typing tool.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Anu fonts Unicode?

No. Anu fonts are legacy glyph-based fonts. The text looks like Telugu only when the same font is installed; underneath it is stored as English character codes.

Can I use Anu fonts in WhatsApp or on my website?

Not directly. The text shows up as English gibberish on other devices. Convert it to Unicode first, or retype it in Unicode.

Does TeluguFontStyle have an Anu to Unicode converter?

No. Legacy Telugu fonts use their own, unpublished character mappings, and a guessed converter would silently produce wrong text. Use a converter made for your specific font family, or retype short text with our Telugu typing tool.
