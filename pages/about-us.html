About TeluguFontStyle

Why this site exists, what it actually does, and who built it.

Our Story

Built Because the
Alternatives Were Annoying

TeluguFontStyle.co.in started in 2026 as the Telugu sister site of HindiFontStyle.co.in. People kept asking for the same thing in Telugu: a quick way to preview stylish Telugu text without a generator buried behind ads, a forced signup, or a handful of old non-Unicode fonts. So I rebuilt the tools from scratch for the Telugu script and its fonts.

Alongside the generator there's a Unicode converter, a Telugu script converter, a set of typing and text tools, and a small blog covering the questions people actually ask about Telugu fonts, from wedding cards to why WhatsApp text sometimes shows up broken. It's still a young site, and I'm adding to it regularly.

Almost every tool on this site works entirely in your browser — nothing you type is uploaded to or stored on our servers, and there's no account to create. (The two exceptions are clearly marked: translation uses Google Translate, and voice typing uses your browser's speech recognition.) That was a deliberate choice, not a marketing line.

Who Runs This Site

Right now, it's just me — no agency, no team of "passionate designers," just one person who cares about Telugu typography and free tools. I handle the design, the code, the font research, and the blog writing myself, which is also why new content goes up in small, steady batches rather than all at once.

What This Site Is Not

It's not a font marketplace, and it's not affiliated with Google, Microsoft, Silicon Andhra, the makers of Anu fonts, or any font foundry. It's an independent tool for previewing, styling, and converting Telugu text, built with publicly available and open-license fonts.

The Person Behind This Site

Hi, I'm Akshay

I built TeluguFontStyle.co.in on my own, starting in 2026. I'm not a big agency or a design studio — I'm one person who kept running into the same problem: wanting a quick, clean way to preview and style Telugu text without wading through ad-heavy websites or downloading sketchy font packs.

I work on the site, the tools, and the blog myself, which means updates come in waves rather than a constant stream — but everything that goes up is something I've actually tested and use myself, whether that's a font converter or a guide on picking fonts for a wedding card.

If you spot a bug, have a font request, or just want to say the tool was useful, the contact page reaches me directly — not a support queue.
